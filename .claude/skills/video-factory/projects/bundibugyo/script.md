# The Ebola Outbreak Nobody Saw Coming

Runtime target 6-7 min · 49 beats

## STORY SPINE

1. **Through-line object — the burial on 27 March.** Three Red Cross volunteers
   in Mongbwalu manage dead bodies on a mission that had nothing to do with
   Ebola, because nobody knew there was an Ebola outbreak. It returns as: the
   invisible start (1) → who the virus actually kills (19) → the people named
   (42) → the seven weeks before anyone knew (46) → the answer (51).
2. **Question asked early, answered last.** Beat 4 asks why the fastest-growing
   Ebola outbreak on record is happening now. Every chapter offers a candidate —
   a missing vaccine, a rare species, a contact-tracing protocol — and each is
   set aside until beat 51.
3. **The reveal (beats 43-47).** The volunteers were infected on 27 March. The
   outbreak was declared on 15 May. Seven weeks in which the thing was already
   moving and no system existed to see it.
4. **Escalation.** the numbers → the speed → what they don't mean → the missing
   vaccine → what replaces a vaccine → why that fails here → the seven weeks.

**THESIS.** The absent vaccine is the headline. The absent administrative and
security capacity is the cause.

## HARD CONSTRAINTS ON THIS SCRIPT

- **Never "the vaccine doesn't work."** Ervebo is licensed against *Zaire*
  ebolavirus and there is no licensed vaccine for *Bundibugyo*. Wording follows
  CEPI: "There are no licensed vaccines for Bundibugyo virus."
- **Superlative:** "fastest-growing on record" and "largest in DRC's history."
  **Never "largest ever"** — West Africa 2014-16 was far larger. CDC's own
  phrase, "second-largest Ebola outbreak on record", is used and attributed.
- **Every CFR carries its denominator and date.** No claim that the virus is
  becoming more lethal — beat 14 states the opposite explicitly.
- **Risk calibration is mandatory** (beats 15-20) and repeats in every Short
  that shows a mortality figure.
- **No death-toll projection. No identifiable patients. No mutation claim.**
- Aid-funding criticism is **attributed reporting**, placed beside the
  commitment figure, with **no causal line drawn to case counts.**

## COLD OPEN — the latest

1. In March, three Red Cross volunteers in eastern Congo were asked to bury the dead. A routine job, unrelated to Ebola.
2. Nobody knew there was an Ebola outbreak, because nobody had found it yet.
3. As of the seventeenth of August, that outbreak has five thousand one hundred and five confirmed cases, and two thousand four hundred and twenty confirmed deaths.
4. It is spreading substantially faster than any Ebola outbreak on record. So why now?
5. The answer most people have heard is that there is no vaccine. That is true. It is not the reason.

## CHAPTER 01 — What is actually happening

6. The outbreak was declared on the fifteenth of May, in Ituri province, north-eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo.
7. It has since reached fifty-four health zones across six provinces. More than ninety-seven percent of cases sit in three of them.
8. This is the first hundred days of five Ebola outbreaks, counted the same way. One of them is not like the others.
9. Congo passed a thousand confirmed cases forty days after the response began. The twenty eighteen outbreak, in the same country, took about two hundred and thirty-five days.
10. This is the largest Ebola outbreak in Congo's history. The previous largest, in twenty eighteen, had three thousand three hundred and seventeen confirmed cases.
11. But it is not the largest ever. West Africa, twenty fourteen to sixteen, had more than twenty-eight thousand. America's CDC calls this one the second largest on record.
12. Two thousand four hundred and twenty of those people have died. A case fatality rate of forty-seven percent, on the seventeenth of August.
13. At the end of July it was forty-four percent. Nobody is claiming the virus has changed. Testing and reporting move that number too.

## CHAPTER 02 — What that number does not mean

14. Forty-seven percent is a frightening number. It describes people diagnosed with this disease, in the places where it is spreading. It does not describe your risk.
15. Ebola is not airborne, and it does not spread by casual contact. Experts do not consider Ebola viruses to have pandemic potential, for exactly that reason.
16. It spreads through direct contact with the blood or body fluids of someone sick, or someone who has died.
17. Which means the people most at risk are those caring for the sick, and those burying the dead.
18. Outside the region there has been one confirmed case, in France. None in the United States. The CDC puts the risk to the American public at low.

## CHAPTER 03 — The headline

19. There is an Ebola vaccine. It has been used at scale, and it works.
20. It is called Ervebo, and it is licensed against Zaire ebolavirus.
21. This outbreak is not Zaire. It is a different species, called Bundibugyo.
22. The vaccine research foundation CEPI puts it plainly. There are no licensed vaccines for Bundibugyo virus.
23. Bundibugyo was identified in Uganda in two thousand and seven. Fifty-six cases. It appeared once more, in Congo, in twenty twelve. Both contained.
24. So no vaccine was ever licensed for it. That is the headline everyone has reported. It is real. It is also not what is driving this.

## CHAPTER 04 — What you do when there is no vaccine

25. Containing Ebola has never actually depended on a vaccine. It depends on something less glamorous. Administration.
26. You find every person an infected patient has been in contact with.
27. You visit each of them, every day, for twenty-one days — the outer edge of the incubation period. You watch for a fever, and isolate before it spreads.
28. And every step assumes something. That you can locate them. That you can reach them safely. That when you knock, they open the door.

## CHAPTER 05 — Why that fails here

29. In eastern Congo, each of those assumptions fails independently.
30. These provinces host around four point four million internally displaced people. People who move cannot be revisited daily for three weeks.
31. The region has been in armed conflict for years, with multiple armed groups active across Ituri and North Kivu.
32. In late July, five response workers were briefly detained by an armed group. Health facilities have been attacked.
33. The CDC lists violence against health workers, and shortages of protective equipment. And one thing harder to fix. Lack of trust, and misinformation.
34. And the region was already hungry. The World Food Programme counts eight point seven million people at crisis levels of hunger in these provinces — before Ebola arrived.
35. The head of the World Health Organization described insecurity and population movement making it, in his words, nearly impossible to trace contacts.
36. Money has been committed. More than five hundred million dollars from the United States alone. Aid organisations say earlier American cuts weakened the response beforehand. Both are on the record.
37. But the shortage that matters is not only money. It is the ability to send someone to a door, safely, every day, for three weeks.

## CHAPTER 06 — The seven weeks

38. Which brings us back to March.
39. The three volunteers were Ajiko Chandiru Viviane, Sezabo Katanabo, and Alikana Udumusi Augustin, of the Congo Red Cross in Mongbwalu.
40. They handled the bodies on the twenty-seventh of March. All three were dead within seven weeks.
41. The first suspected case was recognised on the twenty-fourth of April, in a nurse.
42. Seven weeks separate that burial from the declaration on the fifteenth of May. Seven weeks in which the virus was moving, and no system existed to see it.
43. That is the gap. Not the vaccine. The seeing.

## CHAPTER 07 — What happens now

44. Uganda's share of this outbreak is already over.
45. It stayed in the capital. Twenty cases, no community spread. Declared over on the twenty-eighth of July.
46. So the answer to why now is this. Not because a vaccine is missing, though one is.
47. Because containment is an administrative act, attempted where administration is hardest.
48. There are trials. A treatment trial in Ituri. Two vaccine candidates entered first human trials this summer, in Britain and Canada.
49. None will arrive in time for this outbreak. What has to hold until they do is the knocking on doors.

## THE COUNTS DISAGREE, AND THE FILM PICKS ONE

Two institutions publish different totals for the same week. WHO AFRO, via UN
News on 18 Aug, gave 5,021 confirmed cases and 2,378 deaths. CDC, reporting DRC
ministry-of-health data as of 17 Aug, gives 5,105 and 2,420 — a higher figure on
an earlier date.

That is not an error in either. CDC's own footnote explains it: the DRC health
ministry does not include probable cases in official reporting, and suspected
cases and deaths are "temporarily excluded from the count pending ongoing
investigations and data standardization." National surveillance and WHO's
validated regional figures run on different clocks and different inclusion rules.

**The film uses CDC's numbers throughout and states the date and basis on
screen**, because CDC publishes the confirmed/probable split explicitly, dates
each country separately, and is the source whose page can be shown on screen
without a licensing question. The earlier cut used WHO AFRO's 5,021. Every
number on screen now comes from one source on one basis.

## DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED

- **Any claim that the virus is mutating or becoming more lethal.** The CFR rose
  from 44% on 30 July to 47.4% on 17 August. Beat 14 states outright that this
  can reflect counting rather than the virus, and draws no conclusion.
- **A mutation concern** attributed to local officials. Not sourced to a
  finding, so it is out rather than weakly hedged.
- **MSF treatment-centre locations.** Could not verify to MSF's own release.
- **PARTNERS enrolment numbers.** WHO DON615 says "over 100"; other reporting
  says "over 50". The film gives no number.
- **US travel restrictions on arrivals from DRC.** Real, current, and CDC's own
  — but the film's subject is why the outbreak is growing in Ituri, not what it
  means for American travellers, and leading with border policy would tilt a
  film about Congolese administrative capacity into a threat-to-America frame.
- **The 8.7m / 26.5m hunger figures as an Ebola consequence.** WFP presents them
  as pre-existing conditions the outbreak lands on top of. The film uses them
  that way and makes no claim that Ebola caused them.
- **Any causal claim linking aid cuts to case counts.**

## EDITORIAL NOTE

The film states what happened and when, attributed. It does not assign blame for
the conflict in eastern Congo, does not characterise any armed group beyond
their reported effect on the response, and makes no projection of deaths. The
three volunteers are named once, from IFRC's own statement, without images. No
identifiable patient appears at any point.
