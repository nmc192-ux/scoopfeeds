# Xylitol — v2 script (third person, reported)

**Target 7:45.** From 14:07. Third person throughout: the film reports what the
research found and what its critics said, and carries no view of its own.

**Every beat carries an on-screen source line** (`SRC`). Where a beat states a
finding, the source names study, journal or venue, year and institution. Where a
beat states a limitation, the source names who said it.

**Provenance of the claims:** all figures below are carried over verbatim from
the v1 script, which was researched in an earlier session. Nothing here has been
re-verified against the primary papers in this session — there is no network
access from the authoring environment. Treat the numbers as inherited, not
re-checked.

`ANIM:` marks a beat whose visual is a code-rendered animated diagram (new card
types, §3 of the brief), not found footage.

---

## COLD OPEN — 5 beats

1. Xylitol is a sugar alcohol. It is in chewing gum, toothpaste, peanut butter,
   and a great deal of what is sold as sugar-free.
   `SRC —` *Xylitol · E967 · sugar alcohol*
2. At the European Society of Cardiology congress in Munich, a team from the
   Charité in Berlin presented data on seventeen thousand seven hundred and ten
   people.
   `SRC —` *ESC Congress 2026, Munich · Charité Universitätsmedizin Berlin*
3. Higher blood xylitol tracked with more heart attacks, more strokes, and more
   deaths.
   `SRC —` *ESC Congress 2026 · presented, not yet peer-reviewed in print*
4. The coverage reported the percentage.
   `SRC —` *press coverage, August 2026*
5. Most of it did not report what the researchers themselves said that number
   measures.
   `SRC —` *Nature Medicine 2024 · authors' own statement*

## CH 1 — WHAT WAS PRESENTED — 9 beats

6. Xylitol tastes almost exactly like sugar, barely moves blood glucose, and is
   made from birch or corn.
   `SRC —` *sugar alcohol · ~2.4 kcal/g*
7. It has been in gum and toothpaste for decades, and the dental evidence behind
   that use is good.
   `SRC —` *Cochrane review · xylitol and caries*
8. `ANIM:` The body also makes it. Xylitol is a normal byproduct of glucose
   metabolism, present in blood at all times.
   `SRC —` *endogenous production · pentose phosphate pathway*
9. The Munich analysis drew on two long-running population studies — one
   Canadian, one British. Not cardiac patients. General population.
   `SRC —` *ESC Congress 2026 · two prospective cohorts*
10. Blood xylitol was measured once, participants were sorted into quarters, and
    then followed.
    `SRC —` *ESC Congress 2026 · quartile analysis*
11. Canadian cohort, six years: the top quarter had a fifty-seven per cent
    higher risk of death, heart attack or stroke than the bottom quarter.
    `SRC —` *ESC Congress 2026 · Canadian cohort · 6-year follow-up*
12. British cohort, thirty years: eighteen per cent.
    `SRC —` *ESC Congress 2026 · British cohort · 30-year follow-up*
13. The middle groups sat in between. More in the blood, more events.
    `SRC —` *ESC Congress 2026 · dose-response across quartiles*
14. A dose-response is the pattern that makes epidemiologists take an
    association seriously.
    `SRC —` *Bradford Hill criteria · biological gradient*

## CH 2 — THE PATTERN — 7 beats

15. This is not the first sugar alcohol to produce this signal.
    `SRC —` *chapter*
16. Nature Medicine, 2023: erythritol. Cleveland cohort, eighty per cent higher
    risk. Berlin cohort, a hundred and twenty-one per cent.
    `SRC —` *Witkowski et al. · Nature Medicine · 2023*
17. European Heart Journal, 2024: xylitol, in around three thousand people
    already undergoing cardiac testing, followed three years. Fifty-seven per
    cent.
    `SRC —` *European Heart Journal · 2024 · Cleveland Clinic*
18. Munich, 2026: fifty-seven and eighteen — this time in the general
    population, and over thirty years.
    `SRC —` *ESC Congress 2026*
19. Five cohorts. Two sugar alcohols. Two continents. Every one pointing the
    same way.
    `SRC —` *2023 · 2024 · 2026*
20. The comparisons are not identical. Some compare quarters; one compares
    thirds.
    `SRC —` *differing quantile definitions across studies*
21. That makes it a consistent direction, not a meta-analysis.
    `SRC —` *not pooled · not weighted*

## CH 3 — THE MECHANISM — 7 beats  · animation chapter

22. The proposed mechanism is not cholesterol. It is clotting.
    `SRC —` *chapter*
23. `ANIM:` Platelets are the cells that clump together to seal a wound.
    `SRC —` *platelet aggregation*
24. `ANIM:` Inside an artery that is already narrowed, the same clumping is what
    a heart attack, and most strokes, actually are.
    `SRC —` *atherothrombosis*
25. In the 2024 work, ten healthy volunteers drank thirty grams of xylitol.
    `SRC —` *European Heart Journal · 2024 · interventional arm, n=10*
26. `ANIM:` Within thirty minutes, several measures of platelet reactivity had
    risen.
    `SRC —` *European Heart Journal · 2024*
27. The same appeared with erythritol, in twenty volunteers, at a dose obtainable
    from a single sugar-free drink.
    `SRC —` *Nature Medicine · 2023 · n=20*
28. Thirty grams is the number the rest of this depends on.
    `SRC —` *test dose · 30 g*

## CH 4 — THE THIRTEEN MINUTES — 12 beats  · the centrepiece

29. There is a problem with reading the cohort numbers as a result of diet.
    `SRC —` *chapter*
30. `ANIM:` Xylitol does not persist. Its half-life in blood is about thirteen
    minutes.
    `SRC —` *pharmacokinetics · t½ ≈ 13 min*
31. After the test drink, levels rose as much as a thousandfold — and were back
    to baseline within four to six hours.
    `SRC —` *European Heart Journal · 2024*
32. The cohort blood samples were drawn after an overnight fast.
    `SRC —` *cohort protocol · fasting draw*
33. By the time the needle went in, anything eaten the day before was long gone.
    `SRC —` *t½ ≈ 13 min · 8-12 h fast*
34. `ANIM:` So what was measured was, in the main, xylitol those people's own
    bodies had produced.
    `SRC —` *endogenous, not dietary*
35. That is not an outside criticism. It is what the researchers wrote.
    `SRC —` *European Heart Journal · 2024 · authors*
36. Their words: plasma levels in the observational cohort represent variations
    in endogenous production, and not food intake.
    `SRC —` *European Heart Journal · 2024 · verbatim*
37. That is a materially different claim from the one that reached the public.
    `SRC —` *paper vs coverage*
38. It leaves open that something in a person's metabolism is already going
    wrong, and higher xylitol is one of the ways it shows.
    `SRC —` *reverse causation · confounding by indication*
39. Epidemiology calls that a marker rather than a cause.
    `SRC —` *marker vs cause*
40. A smoke alarm is strongly associated with house fires. It is not what burns
    the house down.
    `SRC —` *association ≠ causation*

## CH 5 — THE ARGUMENT BACK — 8 beats

41. The researchers have an answer to that, and it is a serious one.
    `SRC —` *chapter*
42. The fasting baseline is what the body makes — but what is added to food is
    over a thousand times higher, so a dietary spike might do more, not less.
    `SRC —` *authors' response*
43. Other cardiologists have published objections in the same journal, under
    their own names.
    `SRC —` *European Heart Journal · correspondence*
44. Xylitol was given intravenously to patients in Germany from the nineteen
    seventies, at doses far beyond dietary intake. The recorded harm is a rare
    kidney complication at extreme doses — not heart attacks.
    `SRC —` *clinical IV use · Germany, 1970s*
45. The animal work required xylitol to be injected, because mice barely absorb
    it by mouth.
    `SRC —` *murine model · IP administration*
46. A separate double-blinded study found seven grams of dietary xylitol
    produced no detectable rise in blood levels at all.
    `SRC —` *double-blinded crossover · 7 g*
47. And tooth decay and gum disease are themselves associated with stroke.
    Xylitol reduces both.
    `SRC —` *periodontal disease and cardiovascular risk*
48. The senior author on the erythritol work holds patents with his institution
    on cardiovascular diagnostics.
    `SRC —` *declared competing interests · Nature Medicine 2023*

## CH 6 — THE EXPOSURE — 8 beats

49. Thirty grams was the test dose. A packet is a different scale entirely.
    `SRC —` *chapter*
50. One piece of xylitol gum: between a fifth of a gram and one gram.
    `SRC —` *per-piece xylitol content*
51. Dentists suggest five to ten grams a day for the anti-caries effect.
    `SRC —` *dental recommendation · 5-10 g/day*
52. Thirty grams is, in the researchers' own comparison, about a pint of
    xylitol-sweetened ice cream.
    `SRC —` *authors' comparison*
53. Two pieces of gum is not the experiment. Toothpaste is not either — it is
    spat out.
    `SRC —` *exposure scale*
54. Thirty grams is reachable through bulk xylitol substituted for sugar in home
    baking, or sugar-free tubs and drinks consumed steadily.
    `SRC —` *kitchen-scale exposure*
55. Fifty-seven per cent is a relative number. The presentation did not publish
    the underlying event rates.
    `SRC —` *ESC Congress 2026 · absolute rates not reported*
56. Without them, the absolute risk — how many people per thousand — cannot be
    stated by anyone.
    `SRC —` *relative risk without a base rate*

## CH 7 — THE LABELLING GAP — 4 beats

57. There is a second problem, and it is regulatory rather than cardiological.
    `SRC —` *chapter*
58. Sugar alcohols are not required to be listed individually on a nutrition
    panel.
    `SRC —` *nutrition labelling · sugar alcohols aggregated*
59. A consumer can read the panel correctly, do everything right, and still not
    know how much xylitol a product contains.
    `SRC —` *labelling requirement*
60. An exposure that cannot be measured cannot be chosen.
    `SRC —` *informed choice*

## CLOSE — 4 beats

61. The association is consistent across five cohorts and has a plausible
    mechanism behind it.
    `SRC —` *2023 · 2024 · 2026*
62. What has not been shown is that eating xylitol is what put it in those
    people's blood.
    `SRC —` *no dietary-intake measurement in any cohort*
63. The study that would settle it — measuring what people consume alongside
    what is in their blood, or a trial with hard endpoints — has not been done.
    `SRC —` *open question*
64. Sugar-free was always a claim about sugar. It was never a claim about health.
    `SRC —` *labelling · saccharin · aspartame · sucralose · erythritol · xylitol*
